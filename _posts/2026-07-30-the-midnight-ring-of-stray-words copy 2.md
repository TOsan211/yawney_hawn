The room settles into the quiet hours of 3 AM. Drifting somewhere between sleep and waking, a stray, uninvited word quietly surfaces in my half-conscious mind, my thoughts stumbling as if tripped by a loose syllable.

…Who even uses a phrase like that?

The context is long gone, swept away by the hours in between. I haven't uttered a single syllable of it all day, nor can I fathom where it would even belong. I don't remember why it anchored here, or what conversation it belonged to. It's just the phrase itself, hanging awkwardly in the dark.

And yet, absurdly enough, there is an uncanny sense of rightness to it—as if this were the exact, long-lost word I had been searching for all along.

And as I taste the familiar, slightly dusty syllable, I realize what it is.

It's a stray thought I lost days ago, or one that slipped past my lips in the afternoon rush and wandered off. Or perhaps, it isn't mine at all—just a homeless syllable someone else tried to say today, floating across the dark to perch on my tongue.

Consider "cauliflower," inexplicably popping up in the middle of a tense meeting, or "ephemeral," randomly sticking to the back of a throat while ordering coffee. Are these mere glitches of a tired mind? Or are they the echoes of words swallowed in frustration by someone else across the world?

Like a sudden ring in the quiet hours—one brief insistence, then silence, leaving only the word behind—they travel across midnight circuits to rest, however briefly, on a stranger's tongue.

Seen that way, this uninvited noise is simply a silent message passing through.

There is still no context here to hold it, no sentence to make it stay—at least for now.

And so, tasting the uninvited guest one last time, I hand it back to the dark:
"Bon voyage," my slipped syllables.
May you find a cozy home on a stranger's tongue instead.
