The kitchen is quiet, save for the low hum of the refrigerator and the faint hiss of steam from the kettle. It’s too early for sharp edges, yet here they are—staring up from the plate.

The toast burned, a little.

A few weeks ago, that dark rim would have set the tone for the hours ahead, a stubborn little misstep in the quiet geometry of the morning. I would have spent those first waking moments trying to make peace with the slice, carving carefully around its edges to rewrite the day before it truly began. But looking at it now, the old urge simply fades away in the faint aroma of the toast.

I watched the way the dark rim hugged the corner, stark against the pale center. Maybe those charred edges are just the bread’s way of saying it’s had a long morning, too. After all, it spent its time in the shadows of the pantry, got sliced by an indifferent machine, and then was thrust into the dry heat of the slot. Sometimes, a little burnt edge feels like its own kind of chic.

I dragged the knife across the block, carving off a generous golden square and pressing it into the warm bread. As the butter melted slowly into the grain, it felt as though it were washing away a multitude of quiet, morning sins. As it softened, sliding into the pores and easing the rigid crunch, it filled the gaps between the flaws. The salt and fat smoothed out the sharp notes of the carbon, turning a mistake into a texture.

If the toast gets a pass for its rough edges, the rest of the morning shouldn't have to be pristine either. I let the spoon slide into the bowl one more time, adding a quiet touch of sweetness to the dark roast, accompanied by a small, knowing smile.

Some mornings aren't meant to be optimized. They are meant to be buttered, sweetened, and quietly forgiven.
